Services

What the studio offers

Design consultancy

Syntropic agroforestry designs for farms, institutions, parks and rooftops, large and small, with priority on native Taiwanese species in high-density systems.

Soil assessment

Microbiometer and microscope readings of soil biology, read together with laboratory soil chemistry, to set a baseline and measure change.

Courses and workshops

From a three-hour introduction to two- and five-day immersion courses with hands-on planting.

Biomass and ecosystem management

Pruning plans, planting calendars and on-site guidance that use the life cycles of species to build fertility and structure.

Approach

A living perspective on spatial design

Soil food web as infrastructure

Lasting growth above ground starts beneath the surface. Healthy micro-ecosystems give resilience and natural water retention.

Succession in time

Short-lived pioneers prepare the ground for longer-lived species. Each life cycle hands over to the next, and pruning keeps the system young and productive.

Strata in space

Every plant has a place in the light: emergent, high, medium or low. Filling all strata at high density uses the sunlight completely and builds biomass fast.

Gengfutian Farm · 2024–2026

From rock to food forest

Two years on stony ground, in five steps. Scroll at your own pace.

  1. 2024.11
  2. 2025.09
  3. 2026.03
  4. 2026.07
  5. 2026.09

  1. November 2024

    Gravel and an old orchard

    A ten-year-old soursop orchard on shallow, stony, calcareous ground, with almost no organic matter. The first log lines are laid out on bare gravel.

  2. September 2025

    Soil builds under the tree lines

    Pruned biomass, wood chips and banana trunks are laid along the tree lines. The soil beneath them changes first; the open inter-rows follow later.

  3. Gengfutian: tree line A2 in November 2025 with banana, moringa, taro and cassava, and papaya fruiting among cassava and banana.

    March 2026

    Chili planted, then no more water

    Chili seedlings go into the syntropic beds between the tree lines. After 21 March they receive no irrigation, no fertiliser and no sprays; the trees, mulch and soil life have to carry them.

    Chili is one crop among many. The same lines yield banana, taro, cassava, moringa, soursop and herbs at different times of the year, so the harvest does not depend on a single plant or season, and the system copes better with dry spells and typhoons.

  4. A honeybee on taro and a clearwing moth on Bidens, Gengfutian.

    July–October 2026

    Harvest, and life returns

    Chili plants keep fruiting into October, through the dry months, without irrigation.

    Flowers in every layer feed honeybees, moths and other pollinators. Spiders, wasps and birds hunt among the plants and keep plant-eating insects in balance, so no sprays are needed.

  5. Gengfutian, September 2025 and September 2026. Drag the line or use the arrow keys.

    September 2026

    A closed food forest

    Less than two years after the first logs, the tree lines have grown into a dense, layered food forest. Shade, leaf litter and roots keep the ground cool and covered.

Ching Mei's Food Forest

The landowner had planted tropical and subtropical fruit trees on unused grassland without success. From June 2024 the site was converted into a high-diversity, high-density syntropic food forest. Annual crops filled the early phases; berries and fruit trees are now producing.
