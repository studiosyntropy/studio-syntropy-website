Tzu Chi University in Hualien, Taiwan, has received a Merit Award in the Ecological Common Good category at the [2026 Global Views USR Awards](https://www.tcnews.com.tw/education/item/29063.html) for its initiative “Front Mountain Shangri-La: Resilience and Common Good of Food Forest × Charitable Agriculture”. The award recognises the methods we apply and teach every day.

## From Vulnerability to Resilience

In recent years, Hualien has faced strong earthquakes, typhoons and floods that destroyed farmland and much else besides. We realised that recovering from each disaster was not enough; we needed farming systems able to absorb the next one.

!ba[Before: rows of young trees with logs on stony ground below the mountains|After: the same view as a dense green food forest|Gengfutian, the same view before and after. Drag the line or use the arrow keys.](img/blog/blog-tcu-3.jpg img/blog/blog-tcu-4.jpg)

With syntropic agroforestry, we establish multi-layered, successional systems, and at Gengfutian, our demonstration farm, the results can be measured. In September 2025, topsoil under the tree lines held 10.6–15.1% organic matter, against 1.9% in the adjacent monoculture. In 2026, analyses by the World Vegetable Center found nearly eight times the microbial biomass in the syntropic soil. In a small chili trial the same year, syntropic plots gave 3.14 kg of fruit against 0.60 kg in the control plots, without irrigation after March and without fertiliser or sprays. These systems also support biodiversity, drainage and water-holding capacity, and help regulate the microclimate during extreme weather. Our aim is to help Hualien move from vulnerability towards climate resilience.

## Bridging Traditional Knowledge and Modern Ecology

The work is as much about community as about soil and seeds. The project connects Truku (Taroko) communities in the mountains, Amis (Pangcah) settlements on the coast and farmers on the plains. Working directly with tribal elders, we find that Indigenous farming knowledge and syntropic design complement each other, so that natural cycles and cultural heritage are cared for together.

## A Self-Sustaining Economic Web

Ecological resilience needs economic footing. To move beyond charity, the initiative uses Participatory Guarantee Systems (PGS) and links harvests to consumer cooperatives. Digital tools such as community currency apps help local farmers build a mutually supportive economy that keeps value within the community.

## Taking Syntropy Global

The award is an encouraging milestone. It recognises our work in Taiwan, yet it also reflects the shared vision of the Food Forest Network and the Tzu Chi Foundation’s aim to relieve suffering and foster a more harmonious society through concrete, compassionate action. We see it as an invitation to explore syntropic agroforestry further and to find new partners who share our commitment to agroecology.

Together with our partners, we plan to turn our experience into practical manuals. Learning alongside overseas demonstration sites such as Noble Gateway Malaysia, and a partner site in Mozambique planned from 2027, we aim to translate local experience into models that international projects can use.

When we plant a food forest, we design a more resilient future. Thank you to everyone who is restoring balance and carrying this work forward with us.
